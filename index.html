We are an independent product-design house for people who grow things.

I. WHO WE ARE

A small company with dirt under its nails.

Dirty Jerm's began with a habit: noticing the little things that make plant care more frustrating than it needs to be, then wondering whether they could be designed better.

We are not a nursery. We are not a lifestyle brand pretending every object needs a story. We are a product company built around curiosity, function, and the belief that useful things can still have personality.

We're intentionally small. That means ideas can be tested, broken, changed, and rebuilt without a committee sanding all the character off them.

Useful first. Beautiful because it earned it.

THE MOTTO

Good things get dirty.

To us, dirt is proof that something actually happened.

Roots push through soil. New ideas leave ink in the margins. Prototypes leave scraps on the bench, fingerprints on the parts, and occasionally a few choice words hanging in the air.

We are not interested in mess for mess's sake. We are interested in the kind that comes from doing the work — trying, learning, fixing, growing, and caring enough to get involved instead of standing back and keeping our hands clean.

Clean is nice. Progress usually isn't.

III. WHAT WE BELIEVE

Good design should disappear into the experience.

The best product is not the thing you spend all day admiring. It is the thing that quietly lets something else happen.

A leaf unfurls. A root finds somewhere to hold. A plant climbs instead of collapses. A frustrating routine becomes simple enough that you stop thinking about it.

That is the standard: products should earn their place by enabling something real.

Function before gimmicks.
If it doesn't make the experience better, it doesn't belong.

Evidence before ego.
A prototype is allowed to tell us our favorite idea was stupid.

Personality without nonsense.
We take the work seriously. Ourselves, slightly less so.

IV. THE FOUNDRY

Where ideas have to become real.

The Foundry is Dirty Jerm's product-development workspace: part workshop, part laboratory, part graveyard for ideas that sounded better before they existed physically.

It's where we sketch, model, print, test, document, revise, and occasionally swear at objects until they behave.

We keep the failures because they are part of the work. We keep the wins because they tell us what to do next. And we do not publish the clever parts before they are ready to leave the room.

V. THE HOUSE

Built by a plant person who got tired of accepting “good enough.”

Dirty Jerm's was founded by Jeremiah Anskate, owner, designer, builder, tester, and — when appropriate — Head Cultivator.

The company grew out of equal parts plant obsession, product-design curiosity, 3D printing, and a stubborn belief that everyday problems deserve better answers than “that's just how it is.”

There is no enormous team hiding behind the curtain. Right now, the person asking the question is usually the same person sketching the answer, building the prototype, breaking it, and writing down what happened.

Build carefully. Stay curious. Make things worth making.
